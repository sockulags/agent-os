# Prose tells

Lookups for the failures named in the skill. Sections 1 to 3 apply to English prose. Section 4
applies when the surrounding prose is any other language.

A replacement is a default, not a ban. Keep the flagged word when it is the accurate one: a
`vector` in geometry and a `cache` that is literally a cache are correct.

## 1. Words with a plainer replacement

Elevated synonyms: `utilize` becomes `use`, `leverage` becomes `use`, `facilitate` becomes `help`,
`numerous` becomes `many`, `additionally` becomes `also`, `crucial` becomes `important` or the
consequence itself, `enhance` becomes `improve`, `ensure` becomes `make sure` or the mechanism that
guarantees it.

Fancy ways to say `is`: `serves as`, `stands as`, `acts as`, `boasts`, and `features` all become `is`
or `has`.

Vocabulary that signals generated text: `delve`, `interplay`, `intricate`, `tapestry`, `testament`,
`underscore`, `showcase`, `pivotal`, `garner`, `landscape` used abstractly, `vibrant` used
abstractly. Replace with the plain word or cut the sentence.

Abstract metaphor nouns, with the concrete word they usually stand for: `substrate` becomes `base`,
`wedge` becomes `add`, `vector` becomes `way` or `method`, `surface` becomes `interface` or `API`,
`primitive` becomes the actual type or operation, `nexus` and `locus` become `center` or the named
component, `bedrock` becomes `foundation`, `scaffolding` becomes `setup` or `generated files`,
`north star` and `flywheel` become the goal or the mechanism itself, `gold-plating` becomes `more
than the job needs`, `ratchet` becomes `a limit that only tightens`, `evacuate` becomes `move out`,
`endgame` becomes `the last phase`.

## 2. Phrases to cut outright

Filler that carries no information: `in order to` becomes `to`, `due to the fact that` becomes
`because`, `it is important to note that` is deleted along with everything like it, `at this point in
time` becomes `now`.

Stacked hedges: `could potentially possibly be argued that it might` becomes `may`. One hedge is
honest, three are noise.

Vague attribution: `experts believe`, `industry reports suggest`, `some critics argue`. Name the
source or delete the claim.

Conversational residue: `Great question`, `You are absolutely right`, `Certainly`, `I hope this
helps`, `Let me know if`, `Found the smoking gun`. Delete them and start with the finding.

Disclaimers that substitute for work: `while specific details are limited`, `I was unable to fully
verify`. Go find the detail, or state precisely what is unverified and what that costs the reader.

Generic conclusions: `the future looks bright`, `this positions the project well`. Replace with the
next concrete step or delete the paragraph.

## 3. Punctuation and formatting habits

Dashes as connectors in running text. Split the sentence or use a comma. Reaching for parentheses
instead trades one habit for another.

Colons joining two independent clauses mid-sentence. A colon belongs before a list or an example.
Elsewhere, end the sentence.

Boldface on every proper noun, product name, or acronym. Bold marks the one thing that matters in a
passage; bolding six things marks none of them.

Bold labels that restate the line they introduce, as in `**Performance:** Performance improved`.
Convert to prose. A bold lead-in naming a distinct item followed by genuinely new detail is fine.

Title case headings become sentence case. Decorative emoji in headings and bullets are removed. Curly
quotes become straight quotes.

## 4. Technical terms that stay English

When writing in another language, keep the term a developer says out loud. A translated term the
reader has never heard costs them a translation step before they can act on the sentence.

Keep in English: `edge case`, `pull request`, `commit`, `merge`, `branch`, `rebase`, `deploy`,
`build`, `cache`, `endpoint`, `race condition`, `refactor`, `rollback`, `staging`, `linter`, `mock`,
`fixture`, `feature flag`, `deprecated`, `boilerplate`, `callback`, `throughput`, `scope`.

Translate normally: `databas`, `server`, `fil`, `minne`, `nätverk`, `kod`, `tabell`, `användare`.
These are the words people actually use.

The test is usage, not the dictionary. `kantfall` for `edge case` and `draganmodan` for `pull
request` are inventions no developer says. When unsure, keep the English term.
